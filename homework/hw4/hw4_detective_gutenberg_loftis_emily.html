Part 4 — Visualize It

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

plt.bar(["Jane Austen", "Mary Shelley"],
        [book1_ly_rate, book2_ly_rate])

plt.title("Words Ending in -ly")
plt.xlabel("Author")
plt.ylabel("Matches per 1,000 words")

book1_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in Counter(book1_words).most_common()]
book2_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in Counter(book2_words).most_common()]

book1_ranks = range(1, len(book1_freqs) + 1)
book2_ranks = range(1, len(book2_freqs) + 1)

plt.plot(book1_ranks, book1_freqs, label="Jane Austen")
plt.plot(book2_ranks, book2_freqs, label="Mary Shelley")

plt.title("Word Rank vs. Frequency")
plt.xlabel("Rank")
plt.ylabel("Frequency")
plt.xscale("log")
plt.yscale("log")
plt.legend()
plt.savefig("zipf_comparison.png")
plt.show()

I used matplotlib to make a bar chart comparing ly words for both authors. The author names are the labels, and the rates from part 3 are the heights of the bars. For the Zipf plot, I used counter to put all the words in order from most common to least common. I used pair[1] to get each word’s count and range() to create the ranks. I plotted both books on the same graph and made both axes log scales. I added titles, labels, and a legend and used .savefig() to save both charts. I used ChatGPT to help me fully understand how to use log with the charts.

Part 5 — The Verdict

Like I previously mentioned Austen had higher rates of words ending in ly and ment, while Shelley had higher rates of words ending in ness and words starting with un. Austen’s common words had characters names like Darcy and Elizabeth while Shelley’s included man, father, and life. I think the ly rate was the most useful because there was a noticeable difference between the books. Average word length was the least useful because the results were almost the same. If I had an anonymous chapter, I would check the ly rate first, but I would need more evidence before deciding who wrote it.
